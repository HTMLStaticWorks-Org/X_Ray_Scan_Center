Electrophysiology

Immediate Rhythm Detection. Cardiologist Interpretation.

An electrocardiogram (ECG) records the tiny electrical potentials that travel through heart muscle tissue during every systolic and diastolic cycle.

Our digital 12-lead machines employ active baseline stabilization and muscle tremor artifact filtration, capturing precise P-wave, QRS-complex, and ST-segment contours essential for identifying silent ischemia, arrhythmias, and conduction blocks.

Testing Procedure

Your ECG Appointment Journey

A quick, completely painless 10-minute diagnostic session with immediate rhythm confirmation.

1

Rest & Relaxation

Lie flat and relax for 5 minutes in our quiet cardiac room so your resting heart rate and blood pressure stabilize.

2

Electrode Placement

Six chest electrodes (V1-V6) and four limb electrodes are placed with gentle conductive gel on your chest, arms, and legs.

3

Waveform Recording

Remain still and breathe quietly for 10 to 15 seconds while the machine records synchronized rhythm vectors across 12 channels.

4

Review & Handover

Electrodes are removed gently. The printed rhythm strip and automated interval report are verified by the physician immediately.

Cardiac Electrophysiology

What Your 12-Lead ECG Measures

How individual vector deflections map the electrical activation of your heart muscle in millivolts and milliseconds.

P-Wave Vector

Atrial Depolarization

Originates from the sinoatrial (SA) node. Analyzed for right/left atrial enlargement, ectopic rhythms, and flutter wave morphology.

PR-Interval (120-200 ms)

Atrioventricular Conduction

Measures transit delay through the AV node and bundle of His. Crucial for detecting 1st, 2nd, and 3rd-degree heart blocks.

QRS-Complex (< 120 ms)

Ventricular Depolarization

Rapid electrical activation of the ventricles. Widening indicates bundle branch blocks (LBBB/RBBB) or ventricular ectopy.

ST-Segment & T-Wave

Myocardial Repolarization

Primary indicator of acute myocardial ischemia, STEMI elevations, reciprocal depressions, or electrolyte abnormalities.
